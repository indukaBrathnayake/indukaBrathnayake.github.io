PAD Estimator

A non-invasive, low-cost embedded device for early Peripheral Artery Disease risk estimation using infrared temperature sensing and exercise-based diagnostics.

Overview

What is the PAD Estimator?

Peripheral Artery Disease (PAD) is a circulatory condition where narrowed arteries reduce blood flow to the limbs. Early detection is critical but current clinical methods are expensive and inaccessible. This project developed a portable, low-cost device that estimates PAD risk non-invasively by measuring foot temperature changes before and after a 3-minute walk test.

The device uses dual infrared (IR) temperature sensors on both feet, computes an asymmetry-based risk score incorporating age and gender factors, and displays the result (0–100%) with intuitive LED colour feedback.

How It Works

Measurement Protocol

  1. Enter Age & Gender — Input patient details via the keypad
  2. 5-Minute Rest — Wait for body to reach stable baseline state
  3. Baseline Measurement — Record initial temperature of both feet
  4. 3-Minute Walk — Timed exercise to stress peripheral blood flow
  5. Post-Walk Measurement — Record second temperature reading
  6. Risk Score Display — PAD risk (0–100%) shown on LCD with LED indicator

Risk Score Formula

Risk Score = (30·SAsym + 25·SLeft + 25·SRight + 15·SAge + 5·SGen) / 100

Risk Feedback

LED Indicator System

🔴
High Risk (> 70%)
Red LED — Immediate clinical referral advised
🟡
Medium Risk (40–70%)
Both LEDs — Lifestyle monitoring recommended
🟢
Low Risk (< 40%)
Green LED — Normal, no immediate concern

Technical Challenges

Key Engineering Solutions

1-Wire Communication via Assembly

The ATmega328P lacks native 1-Wire hardware support. A custom Assembly-level driver was developed with cycle-counted delays — exactly 16 clock cycles per microsecond at 16 MHz — ensuring reliable DS18B20 sensor communication.

Latency & Arithmetic Optimisation

Sensor conversion latency (750ms) was handled with staged delays. Risk formula arithmetic was converted to integer-scaled operations to maintain precision within 8-bit Assembly limitations.

IR Foot Presence Detection

Dual IR sensors verify both feet are correctly placed before measurement begins. Continuous monitoring detects movement and restarts the process, preventing false readings.

Power Optimisation — Sleep Mode

IR sensors are powered via a 2N2222 transistor-based switching circuit, active only during detection phases. This reduces current draw and minimises electrical noise affecting temperature accuracy.

Built-in Self-Test

System Validation

A comprehensive self-test routine can be triggered anytime by holding 'C' on the keypad. It validates temperature sensors, IR sensors, I²C devices (LCD & keypad), LEDs, and the buzzer — with real-time step-by-step feedback on the LCD display.
